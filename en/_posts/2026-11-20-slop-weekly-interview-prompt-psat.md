Three new posts went up on [Slop](https://slop.ai.kr/) this week. To be upfront, I posted all three. So this roundup is closer to "why I posted these and what I want judged," and at the end I added two posts that other people pushed up with gem votes.

## A prompt that interviews you before it writes

Ask an AI for a thank-you note and you get something polished that you could send to anyone. [This prompt](https://slop.ai.kr/p/77cd0d8b-d488-43e6-82e9-4ddd781d1abc/) flips the order: the AI asks you up to six questions, one at a time, and only then drafts the note in your words. The key part is telling it to leave a blank instead of inventing anything you didn't say. The downside is that it's slow, and overkill for a two-line message. The post also admits I haven't measured whether it actually beats a plain prompt. I'd most like to hear from anyone who tries it on an apology or a wedding toast.

## A PSAT mock exam with 3,915 past questions

The PSAT is the aptitude test used to screen candidates for Korea's civil service. This [mock exam site](https://slop.ai.kr/p/05501232-1492-4a45-bca5-8f87f2cc607f/) (in Korean) cuts 117 past papers from 2005 to 2026 into single questions and lets you take them with a timer and an OMR answer sheet. Claude wrote the explanations by looking at each question image, and along the way it flagged that the 2005 answer key didn't match the order of the questions. That was the fun part. I wrote up how it was built in an [earlier post](https://jungtaejoon.github.io/blog/en/2026/11/18/alltests-psat-mock/).

## Time Sense: 10 seconds without a clock

[Tap to start, then tap again when you think 10 seconds have passed](https://slop.ai.kr/p/467f9a5d-f560-47fa-8bc8-6feb41078077/). After three tries it tells you whether you tend to tap late or early. Counting in your head makes it easy, so the post leaves that to your conscience. I wanted to see whether a tiny toy like this can end up a gem.
