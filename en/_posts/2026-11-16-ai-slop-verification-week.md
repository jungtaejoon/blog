Building [Slop](https://slop.ai.kr/) means I keep an eye on news about things made with AI. Putting together a few stories from the past two weeks, I noticed something they share. The conversation is moving past "there's too much AI stuff" toward **who actually checks it**.

## My paper that I didn't write

According to a Times Higher Education investigation republished by [Inside Higher Ed](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/faculty/books-publishing/2026/11/13/ai-slop-papers-published-under-prominent-academics-names) on November 13, a few little-known journals have been running low-quality, apparently AI-generated papers credited to well-known professors who never wrote them. One professor first learned about a paper under his name from a Google Scholar alert, and after he reported it, the same text was reported to have reappeared under other authors' names. The article says names of prominent AI researchers were also listed as authors.

What worries me more than the volume of AI writing is that **names used to stand in for trust**, and that is breaking down. Trusting a paper because of who's on the byline no longer works.

## Let the machine check the math

There's movement in the other direction too. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/axiom-math-wants-to-keep-ai-slop-out-of-mathematics/) reported on November 5 that the startup Axiom Math says its AI agents formally verified the Stacks Project, a large reference work in algebraic geometry, in the proof assistant Lean. With AI-"solved" proofs piling up, the idea is to have a machine confirm they're correct instead of people reading each one line by line.

## Developers say "No AI Slop"

[Latent Space](https://latent.space/p/ainews-the-no-ai-slop-manifesto/) reported that the [No AI Slop Manifesto](https://slopmanifesto.com/) was introduced in the closing keynote of AIE CODE, an AI engineering conference, and cosigned by many of the speakers. I found it interesting that the people using AI the most are the ones starting to name a quality bar.

## The barrier to making keeps dropping

Meanwhile, making things keeps getting easier. Per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/automattic-debuts-a-platform-to-deploy-vibe-coding-apps-amid-a-changing-internet/), Automattic, the company behind WordPress.com, launched Spacefast on November 9: a way to deploy vibe-coded sites and apps with a database, sign-in and email attached. It can be hooked up to agents like ChatGPT, Claude and Cursor. The easier it is to ship, the more there will be.

## How I see it from Slop

Put the four stories side by side and the picture is clear. Making costs next to nothing, and you can no longer trust a name or a polished surface. What's left is **checking for yourself**. Math can be checked by a machine like Lean, but whether a web app is useful or a piece of writing is worth reading still has to be judged by someone who tried it.

That's what I'm trying to do with Slop. Instead of asking who made something, the people who tried it judge it ▲gem or ▼slop and leave a comment on why. It's a way to share out the checking that machines can't do. If you want to try it, open any post on the [judge page](https://slop.ai.kr/#/judge).
